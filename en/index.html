InnerBeat Metronome

Android app

On Google Play · 40 users in 11 countries

My internal sense of tempo isn't steady: as soon as I turn the metronome off, I start to drift. Ordinary metronomes just make sound, so I built one that helps you practice until you no longer need it.

  • Silent bars: it plays for a few bars, then goes silent for a few. During the silent bars there is no cue on screen at all, so you have to keep count yourself
  • Tap-along scoring: tap the screen along with the beat to see, in real time, how many milliseconds early or late you are, plus stats on how steady you are; touch latency calibration is built in
  • Per-beat subdivisions: from quarter notes to sextuplets, every note can be set individually to accent, medium, soft, or rest
  • Change the tempo and time signature during playback without interrupting the beat; a native audio engine keeps it from drifting over long sessions

GoodTower

Android app

Used regularly by my family

Draw a box around a game's health bar on screen, and it sends a notification as soon as it detects the health dropping, so you don't have to keep watching the screen.

  • A draggable floating toolbar lets you select the detection area directly on top of the game
  • Health bars have irregular shapes and the boxes people draw vary in size, so instead of a fixed pixel threshold it uses a rolling maximum of the red-pixel count as the baseline and looks at the relative drop
  • It only triggers after several consecutive frames fall below the threshold, which filters out false alarms from attack animations covering the bar; after triggering it goes into a cooldown to avoid a burst of notifications
  • The detection logic is a pure Kotlin state machine that can be unit tested; the interface is available in Chinese and English

DeskPet

Android app · A pet for your phone screen

In real use

A character that floats on your phone screen, walks around on its own, talks, and responds when you interact with it.

  • It glides with inertia after you drag it; double-tapping and long-pressing trigger different actions, and it can be switched to stay in one place
  • It detects which app is open and follows a schedule to decide whether to appear or hide; it can also be forced to show
